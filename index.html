What happened?

Pacific Northwest heat

  • Record temperatures affected western Oregon and the broader Pacific Northwest.
  • Communities faced serious health risks and opened cooling centers.
  • Plants, trees, infrastructure, and landscapes experienced heat stress.
Climate connection

A warmer climate raises the temperature baseline, making extreme heat more likely and more intense.

Evidence meter: Strong

Scientists have high confidence in the link between human-caused warming and extreme heat.

Tennessee flash flooding

  • More than 20 inches of rain fell within 24 hours near Waverly.
  • Flash flooding devastated homes, roads, and communities.
  • This was a very different summer hazard from Pacific Northwest heat.
Climate connection

Warmer air can hold more water vapor, increasing the potential for heavier downpours when storms form.

Evidence meter: Strong

Heavy precipitation has increased in many parts of the United States, although changes differ by region.

Compare seasons

What happened?

Winter ice storm

  • Freezing rain coated roads, trees, and power lines with ice.
  • Falling limbs and damaged equipment caused widespread outages.
  • Some communities remained without power for days.
Climate connection

A warming climate does not eliminate winter weather. Cold conditions still occur, while winter hazards may change in timing, location, or character.

Evidence meter: Still evolving

Scientists are still learning how climate change affects freezing rain and ice storms in particular regions.

Summer extreme heat

  • Temperatures reached levels far outside typical summer conditions.
  • The heat affected people, infrastructure, plants, and ecosystems.
  • The summer hazard contrasted sharply with the winter ice storm.
Climate connection

Higher average temperatures make unusually hot conditions more likely and more intense.

Evidence meter: Strong

The connection between human-caused warming and extreme heat is well established.

Cold extremes

Why does climate change affect extreme weather?

Climate warms→Weather develops in changed conditions→Some extremes become more likely or intense

Climate change does not affect every hazard equally or produce the same result in every region.

Evidence meter: Strong for the larger pattern

Scientists assess each type of extreme weather separately because the evidence and mechanisms differ.

Average conditions+Seasonal & regional variation+Extreme-weather patterns=Climate

Key takeaway

An individual extreme event is weather. Patterns in the types, timing, location, frequency, and intensity of extreme weather over many years are part of climate. As climate changes, the conditions in which weather develops also change.
