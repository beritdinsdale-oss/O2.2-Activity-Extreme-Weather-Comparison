Now connect the weather to climate

These short explainers show two different ways a warming climate can change the conditions in which weather develops.

☀️

Extreme heat

Warmer baseline, higher odds

Average temperatures rise over time.
Hot weather begins from a warmer starting point.
Extreme heat becomes more likely and can become more intense.
Evidence meter: Strong

The connection between human-caused warming and increasing extreme heat is well established.

🌧️

Heavy precipitation

Warmer air, more available moisture

A warmer atmosphere can hold more water vapor.
More water can be available when storms develop.
That added moisture can contribute to heavier precipitation.
Evidence meter: Strong

Heavy precipitation has increased across much of the United States, although the amount of change differs by region.

Step back and look across decades

A single event is weather. Long-term observations reveal whether the pattern itself is changing.

1960s→1980s→2000s→Today
☀️

Extreme heat

Heat waves have become hotter, occur more often, and tend to be larger and longer-lasting.

Heavy precipitation

Since the 1950s, heavy precipitation has been happening more often across the contiguous United States, with regional differences.

Cold extremes

Extreme cold events have generally become less frequent and less intense across much of the United States.

Source: Fifth National Climate Assessment

Why this matters

Scientists assess each hazard separately. Some connections are very strong; others remain more complex or region-specific.

What belongs in climate?

🌡️Average conditions

Typical temperature and precipitation

📍Regional patterns

Different places can experience different extremes

📅Seasonal patterns

Different extremes occur at different times of year

⚡Extreme-weather patterns

Frequency, intensity, duration, and timing over many years

Key takeaway

An individual extreme event is weather. When scientists examine the types, timing, location, frequency, and intensity of extreme weather over many years, those patterns are part of climate. As climate changes, the conditions in which weather develops also change.
