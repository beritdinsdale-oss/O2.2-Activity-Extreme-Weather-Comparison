How could the same summer of 2021 bring extreme heat to one region and record flooding to another?

Held constant: Summer 2021   Changed: Region and extreme-weather type
☀

Pacific Northwest · Summer 2021

Extreme heat

What happened?

  • Record-breaking heat affected Oregon, Washington, and nearby areas.
  • Communities opened cooling centers and faced serious health risks.
  • Infrastructure, trees, landscapes, and gardens experienced heat stress.

Climate connection

A warmer climate raises the temperature baseline. When a hot weather pattern develops, it can produce more intense and record-breaking heat.

Evidence meter

Strong evidence

Scientists have high confidence that human-caused warming increases the likelihood and intensity of extreme heat.

Waverly, Tennessee · Summer 2021

Record rainfall and flash flooding

What happened?

  • More than 20 inches of rain fell in parts of Middle Tennessee within 24 hours.
  • Flash flooding devastated Waverly and surrounding communities.
  • The event illustrates a very different summer hazard from Pacific Northwest heat.

Climate connection

Warmer air can hold more water vapor. When storms form, that added moisture can contribute to heavier downpours and greater flood risk.

Evidence meter

Strong evidence

Scientists have strong evidence that heavy precipitation has increased in many parts of the United States, though the amount of change differs by region.

How can one region experience very different extreme weather during different seasons?

❄

Willamette Valley · Winter 2021

Ice storm

What happened?

  • Freezing rain coated trees, roads, and power lines with ice.
  • Falling limbs and damaged equipment caused widespread outages.
  • Some Willamette Valley communities remained without power for days.

Climate connection

A warming climate does not eliminate winter weather. Cold and freezing conditions still occur, while the timing, location, and characteristics of winter hazards may change.

Evidence meter

Evidence still evolving

Researchers are still learning how climate change affects freezing rain and ice storms in particular regions.

Willamette Valley · Summer 2021

Extreme heat

What happened?

  • Temperatures reached levels far outside normal summer conditions.
  • The heat affected people, infrastructure, plants, and ecosystems.
  • This summer hazard contrasted sharply with the winter ice storm.

Climate connection

Summer extreme heat has a clear connection to a warming climate: higher average temperatures make unusually hot conditions more likely.

Evidence meter

Strong evidence

The evidence linking human-caused warming with more frequent and intense extreme heat is much stronger than the evidence for ice storms.

Why does climate change affect extremes?

  1. The climate warms. Average conditions shift.
  2. Weather develops in those changed conditions.
  3. Probabilities shift. Some extremes become more likely or intense.
  4. Scientists observe the pattern over decades.

Climate change does not affect every hazard equally or produce the same result in every region.

Evidence meter: the larger pattern

Strong evidence

Long-term observations provide strong evidence that human-caused climate change is altering the frequency or intensity of several kinds of extreme weather, especially extreme heat and heavy precipitation. Scientists assess each hazard separately because the evidence and mechanisms differ.

The three comparisons reveal different parts of the same idea.

1

One region can experience different extreme weather during different seasons.

3

Across time

Average conditions + seasonal and regional variability + patterns of extreme weather = Climate

Key takeaway

An individual extreme event is weather. Patterns in the kinds, timing, location, frequency, and intensity of extreme weather over many years are part of climate. As climate changes, the conditions in which weather develops also change—but scientists are more confident about some extreme-weather connections than others.
